Sources

Each part of the model has earlier work behind it. This page lists what each source supports. The model brings the parts together for classrooms and gives teachers tests to run.

Gathered 25 November 2026. Titles are the sources' own.

AI moves effort to checking

Seeing real value from AI depends on being able to verify its outputs
MIT Sloan

Makes the case that AI makes work cheap to produce but not cheap to check.

AI Does Not Remove Cognitive Load, It Moves It
Substack

Argues that AI moves mental effort from one part of a task to another. A writer becomes a reviewer.

The Verification Tax: Why AI Time Savings Shrink Once Someone Checks the Work
Rework

Collects survey evidence that time moves from creating to checking.

Time saved by AI partially canceled out by time spent checking AI
Accounting Today

Reports survey data on time saved by AI and time spent checking it.

AI's grand promise: Less drudgery, more complexity, same (or lower) pay
The Register

Reports on researchers who expect work to shift from doing tasks to overseeing AI.

From Effort Reduction to Effort Management: An Expectancy Theory Perspective on Professionals' Work Practices with Generative AI
Springer, Business & Information Systems Engineering

Interviews with 21 knowledge workers. Most did not simply reduce effort. They reallocated it or increased it.

AI Debt: Verification Burdens, Rework Externalities, and the Hidden Costs of Asymmetric Generative-AI Adoption
Cambridge Open Engage (preprint)

Argues that AI adoption should be measured across a whole workflow, not only by one person's time saved.

Effort displacement
effortdisplacement.com

A related site on the same core idea.

When to check and where to check

More Rejective, Not More Discriminative: The Unit of Verification in Pre-Execution LLM Oversight
arXiv (preprint)

Tests how much of an AI system's plan a reviewer should inspect at once. Longer reviews catch more errors and also reject more good work.

How to implement effective review gates for AI-assisted development
Thoughtworks

Describes stopping the AI at set points for human review, at two frequencies.

The work is how you learn

Universities must manufacture the work experience AI is deleting
Times Higher Education

Argues that graduates may lack the experience to know when AI is wrong if entry-level tasks go to AI.

AI ate the first rung of the technology career ladder
TechTarget

Argues that AI doing routine tasks may deprive junior workers of the chance to learn.

Designing judgment: How AI reshapes junior & senior collaboration in legal
Thomson Reuters Institute

Looks at how junior lawyers learn to reason when AI changes the first-pass work.

Never-skilling: the research says juniors using AI never learn to debug
The Next Web

Reports a randomized trial of 52 mostly junior engineers. The group given an AI assistant scored lower on a quiz about code they had just written.

Student competency and AI literacy

Framing, Judging, Steering: An Assessable Competency Model for Teaching Students to Reason With Generative AI
arXiv (preprint)

A student competency model with three skills: framing, judging, and steering. It treats evaluating AI output as a skill of its own.

New AI Literacy Framework helps schools prepare learners for the age of artificial intelligence
European Commission and OECD

The AILit Framework for primary and secondary education. Four dimensions: engage with, create with, manage, and shape AI. 19 competences. Presented 18 June 2026.

AI competency framework for students
UNESCO

12 competencies across four dimensions and three levels: Understand, Apply, and Create. Stresses critical evaluation of AI solutions.

AI Moves Work Model by Jason Weimer. jasonweimer.com
